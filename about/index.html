About

James Sanborn

I'm not a contractor who learned design. I'm a designer who builds.

Background

I learned construction from my father, a retired Bay Area architect. Growing up, I was on job sites before I could drive. But I wasn't just learning how to swing a hammer — I was learning how to see buildings. How to understand proportion, light, materiality, and context.

That education was informal but thorough. While other kids were at summer camp, I was learning why a roofline matters, how to detail a window, and what makes good joinery. My father didn't just teach technique — he taught the why behind every decision.

After years working on projects throughout the Bay Area, I moved north to Humboldt County. The work here is different — more restoration, more regional materials, more weather to contend with — but the principles are the same. Good design, honest materials, careful craft.

Why Ferndale

Ferndale has one of the highest concentrations of Victorian architecture on the West Coast. These aren't museum pieces — they're working homes that need thoughtful maintenance and restoration. That work requires someone who understands both historical integrity and modern building science.

Beyond restoration, there's a real appetite here for new work that respects its context. Not fake Victorians, but contemporary buildings that understand scale, proportion, and place. That's the kind of work I want to do.

The Work Today

Sanborn Design is intentionally small. It's me, occasionally a skilled carpenter I've worked with for years, and a carefully selected network of local tradespeople I trust completely.

I take on 6-8 projects a year. That's by design. More would mean less direct involvement, and direct involvement is the whole point. You hire me for the design eye and attention to detail — so you get me, start to finish.

The projects I'm drawn to are ones where design thinking makes a real difference. Historic restorations where preservation requires understanding the original intent. Renovations where spatial decisions matter. New builds where material choices and detailing define the outcome.

Family

I live in Ferndale with my wife and two kids. When I'm not on a job site, you'll find me hiking the Lost Coast, fixing up our 1920s Craftsman, or teaching my kids the same lessons my father taught me — how to see buildings, how to work with materials, and why craft matters.
